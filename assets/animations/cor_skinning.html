Centers of Rotation Skinning

The centers of rotation skinning is in the engine and off by default. This page is for the project that turns it on and for the render team that owns it: what is in the engine, how to enable it, what it costs, where it stops and how to check it.

Le and Hodgins, “Real-time Skeletal Skinning with Optimized Centers of Rotation”, SIGGRAPH 2016. A per-vertex center c is precomputed from the rest pose and the weights. At runtime the vertex rotates by the weighted blend of the bone rotations q around c, and c itself moves with linear blend skinning (LBS): v' = R(q) (v - c) + LBS(c). A vertex whose center offset is zero (one bone, or no triangle shares two of its bones) keeps its LBS position; its normal takes the blended rotation, which equals the LBS one for a single bone.

What Is in the Engine

  • Project switch: cor_skinning_support, an interval in prog/daNetGame/shaders/skinning_inc2.dshl that each shader build assumes. assume_if_not_assumed keeps it off, so a project that does nothing gets the shaders, the variants and the vertex channels it had before. A project turns it on with cor_skinning_support:i=1 in the assume_vars of every shader build that compiles the dynamic shaders: the game dumps, the Asset Viewer dump and the exporter (tools) dump. All three must agree, because cooked vertex data is matched against the shader channel list (ScriptedShaderMaterial::checkChannels): the exporter dump decides whether the builder writes the channel, the game dump whether the shaders read it.

  • Shaders: with the switch on, every daNetGame dynamic shader that expands DYNAMIC_SIMPLE_VS_INPUT (the character shaders dynamic_skin, dynamic_masked_chrome_bump, dynamic_hair, dynamic_painted_by_mask among them) declares the color8 tc[8] = extra[60] channel in its skinned variant (num_bones == four_bones, prog/daNetGame/shaders/dynamic_simple_inc.dshl). A dynamic shader with its own channel list, such as dynamic_layered, does not take part. skinning_inc2.dshl blends the four bone quaternions (antipodal sign fixed against the heaviest bone) and applies the formula for the current and the previous frame. A zero scale bone gives no rotation to the blend, and a vertex with a weight on it stays on LBS. A part the node collapser hides vanishes whole through the collapser bits. No shader gets a new variant: the project switch is assumed.

  • Bone rows: with the switch on, dynrend (prog/gameLibs/render/dynmodelRenderer), which fills the bone rows of daNetGame and of War Thunder, sends a bone as three rows of rotation quaternion, position and scale, not as the rows of its 3x4 matrix (calc_bone_tm). The row count does not change, and the animation pipeline keeps its matrices: dynrend decomposes each bone matrix when it fills the rows. The daNetGame animchar update builds the rows of a skin once in a frame (cache_animchar_skin_bone_rows) and keeps them in the scene instance; each pass copies them. A node write after that drops the kept rows, and the passes decompose again. Every skinned shader of the project reads this form. A skinned shader without the channel takes the same path with a zero center, and the code that needs a skinning matrix (cloth, eyes, eight-bone skins, the non-batched ray tracing prepass) builds it from the three rows.

  • War Thunder: its own dynamic shaders in skyquake/prog/shaders/source (dynamic_simple_inc.dshl, skinning_inc2.dshl and the classes that expand its DYNAMIC_SIMPLE_VS_INPUT with use_skinning) take the same project switch, channel, bone rows and formula. Both projects share the math in prog/gameLibs/render/shaders/cor_skinning.hlsl.

  • Builder: prog/tools/libTools/shaderResBuilder/corSkinning.cpp computes the centers (similarity sigma 0.1, edge subdivision by weight distance 0.1, up to 3 levels, sub triangles grouped by quantized weight vector). The builder in shSkinMeshData.cpp runs it for every skin whose material declares extra[60] in the exporter dump, on every Level of Detail (LOD), with the same quantized 4-bone weights the shader sees, and writes extra[60] per mesh vertex as four bytes: c - v as three signed bytes, times the scale 2^((w - 200) / 8) of the fourth byte (encode_cor_offset; the shaders read it with cor_offset_decode). No option in application.blk, in the asset properties or in a DAG node selects it: the exporter dump does.

  • Channel ID: prog/dagorInclude/shaders/dag_corSkinningChannel.h.

  • Ray tracing: the batched bounding volume hierarchy (BVH) skinned prepasses of daNetGame and War Thunder read the same channel (prog/gameLibs/bvh/bvh.cpp channel parser, bvh_processors.cpp, prog/daNetGame/shaders/rt/bvh_process_skinned_vertices.dshl, skyquake/prog/shaders/source/bvh_process_skinned_vertices_batched.dshl), so the bottom-level acceleration structure (BLAS) matches the raster mesh.

How to Enable in a Project

  1. Turn the project switch on in assume_vars that every shader build of the project includes, the game, Asset Viewer and exporter dumps alike, so that they agree: game_common_override.blk in Active Matter and Enlisted, common_assumes_wt.blk in War Thunder:

    cor_skinning_support:i=1

    The Enlisted Oculus dump (shaders_spirv_oculus.blk) does not include game_common_override.blk, so it takes the line in its own file.

    Three War Thunder game dumps do not include common_assumes_wt.blk: the PS5 compatibility dump (shaders_ps5_compatibility.blk), the iOS compatibility dump (shaders_metal_ios_compatibility_wt.blk) and the Xbox One dump (shaders_xboxOne.blk). They take their assumes from common_assumes_compatibility.blk or common_assumes.blk, which War Thunder Mobile or VR Tanks share, so each takes the line in its own file. The VR Tanks Scarlett dump (shaders_dx12xs_vrt.blk) includes shaders_xboxOne.blk, so it removes the line again with "@delete:cor_skinning_support":i=0 in its common_assume_vars override.

  2. Recompile the game shaders, the Asset Viewer dump and the exporter dump.

  3. Rebuild every skinned dynmodel: daBuild-dev.exe -target:PC -rebuild:dynModel application.blk. Land the rebuilt assets together with the game shaders: a skin cooked without the channel does not match a shader that declares it.

Rollout: Active Matter first (fewer outfits), then Enlisted and War Thunder. A project that does not need it (War Thunder Mobile, for example) does nothing. Owner: render.

Cost

  • Vertex data: +4 bytes per vertex on every skinned LOD whose material declares the channel.

  • Vertex shader: per application, one offset decode, one quaternion blend, one quaternion to matrix and four vector rotations. The skinned dynamic_simple vertex shader of Active Matter grows from 413 to 621 DXIL operations. The normal pass applies it twice, for the current and the previous frame (motion vectors); the depth and shadow passes once.

  • Bone rows on the CPU: the decomposition takes 12 ns for a bone, against 6 ns for its matrix rows (clang, SSE4.1, one thread). The daNetGame animchar update (animchar_before_render_es) decomposes a bone once in a frame (twice in a frame that has no previous rows of the skin mesh: the first frame, a LOD switch) and each pass copies the rows: in the benchmark the bone rows of a character (3.1 passes of 69 bones) take 7.8k CPU cycles in a frame, against 7.2k for matrix rows and 14.1k when each pass decomposes. The kept rows take 96 bytes for each bone of a skinned animchar. An animchar that another system updates (the damage model animchar, for example) and War Thunder decompose in each pass.

  • Build: well under a second per LOD. The similarity of a sub triangle to a vertex depends on the weights alone, so the builder sums the sub triangles of one quantized weight vector into one group. Measured on the Active Matter trooper LOD0: the 10k-vertex torso skin takes about 0.2 s, the whole character LOD0 plus LOD1 under 0.5 s. The builder logs cor skinning: N of M verts have a non-zero center offset, max/avg, weight groups, seconds per skin.

  • Resource size in Active Matter: with the switch on, every skinned dynmodel carries the channel on every LOD, and the PC resource packs that hold them grow by 37 MB, from 1.88 to 1.92 GB (+2.0%), of about 6.4 GB of resource packs in all. The 413 am_* character packs take 35 MB of it (+10% of their 341 MB); the rest are weapon, gear and loot models and destructible cloth props (*_destr). The LODs after LOD1 add little, because a far LOD has few vertices. The dedicated server packs do not change, because the server export skips dynmodels.

  • Measured frame cost: the MotionMatching benchmark (gamedata/scenes/cor_skinning_bench.blk, launcher samples/MotionMatching/game/motion_matching_cor_bench.bat) renders 100 survivors (14k mesh vertices at LOD0; the grid mixes LOD0 and LOD1) at 1920x1080 and writes the GPU time of the passes to the log, for the build it runs in: an A/B compares a sample build with the switch on against one with it off. Each phase gives the profiler avg of a pass (GPU columns) and the mean CPU animation time of its 6 s measure. The table is the median of the six phases of a build from three runs, with the run order off, on, on, off, off, on, on an RTX 5070 Ti Laptop GPU, DX11:

Build GPU frame G-buffer dynamics Shadow cascades CPU animation
Switch off (linear blend) 2.432 ms 0.341 ms 0.587 ms 0.147 ms
Switch on (centers of rotation) 2.409 ms 0.333 ms 0.597 ms 0.141 ms

The GPU cost of the centers is inside the noise of the runs (about 3%) for 100 characters. CPU animation does not change. The survivor resource grows by 12% (87 KB: 4 bytes for each of the 21.6k vertices of LOD0 and LOD1, more than the mesh vertices because of the UV and normal seams), and by 14% (60 KB) as the zstd-compressed resource on disk.

Known Limits

  • A bone goes to the shaders as rotation, position and scale, so a bone matrix with shear loses the shear, in every skinned shader of a project with the switch on. Bones carry no shear.

  • The four-byte offset keeps seven bits and a sign for each axis, against the largest axis of the offset: the stored center is off by 0.3% of the offset length on average and by 0.8% at most (0.16 mm at 6 cm, up to 1.6 mm at 23 cm). The skinned position moves by that times the difference between the blended matrix and the blended rotation, which is zero in the bind pose.

  • A non-uniform bone scale affects only the LBS part (the center), not the rotated offset. Characters do not scale bones non-uniformly.

  • Projective wounds rewrite the bone weights of cut vertices at runtime. The precomputed center stays, so the cut region is an approximation. Those vertices lie inside the wound geometry.

  • Adds no volume. Deltoid or pectoral bulge needs helper bones or corrective shapes, which is out of scope.

  • A skin with more than 4 bones per vertex (setBonePerVertex above 4) gets zero offsets, so it keeps the LBS position; the builder logs a warning for it.

  • No runtime off switch: a project checks the centers before it ships them. Turning them off takes the project switch off and an asset rebuild.

  • Only the batched BVH skinned prepasses read the channel. The non-batched prepass keeps LBS for the ray-traced copy.

Validation

  1. The MotionMatching sample, scene gamedata/scenes/cor_skinning_test.blk (samples/MotionMatching/game/motion_matching_cor_skinning.bat): the survivor with the centers, looping one clip under a long-lens camera. The ImGui panel “CoR skinning” (F2 to click it) picks the clip (reload, rifle sprint, sprint, crouch walk, injured walk, rifle idle; each bends other joints) and sets the time speed; F8 pauses and resumes, Shift+F8 switches slow motion, F9 takes a screenshot, F10 frees the camera (WASD, mouse, E and C, Z and Q). Compare the shots with a sample build that has the switch off in sample_shaders_override.blk, with its game, Asset Viewer and exporter dumps recompiled and its skinned dynmodels rebuilt. Keep the shots of each build in their own folder: a shot’s file name holds only the capture time.

  2. Asset Viewer with the project’s application.blk: open a soldier dynmodel, bend the arm with an animation, compare elbow, shoulder and knee with a build that has the project switch off.

  3. In game: the same comparison, plus motion vectors (temporal anti-aliasing ghosting on moving characters) and ray-traced shadows or reflections on characters.

  4. Compare the built resource sizes and the build log of a full dynmodel rebuild before and after enabling, for size and time; the vertex count does not change, the channel adds 4 bytes per vertex.
