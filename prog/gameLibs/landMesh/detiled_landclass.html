De-tiling landclasses

A tile texture repeats, and the eye finds the grid. De-tiling shows the same tile as up to eight turned and shifted copies, and blends between them. You set three keys in the land.blk. The level data does not change, and the trees and rocks the landclass plants from a tile or a density map move with the texture.

1 · What you get

The same texture, without the grid

Drag the line. Left of it the landclass tiles the plain way. Right of it the same tile is de-tiled with four clusters, which is where you start. Turn on tile grid to see the period, or press Blink to flip the whole frame between the two, the way you would check any texture fix.

Plate 1 · plain tiling vs de-tiled 3600 m across - tile 512 m - 4 clusters

The tile is drawn in your browser, so it is not a shipping asset. The de-tiling map is the engine's own: this page runs the same hash, the same 64×64 noise lookup table and the same cluster math as the shader and the object generators.

What it costs you: one noise sample per pixel, a second texture tap inside the blend band, and a tile that has to be stochastic. What it costs the level: nothing. The keys travel inside the landclass asset, so daBuild and the editor copy them across with no re-export of the level and no change to the binary format.

Pixels cost the same from two clusters up, since a pixel reads at most two clusters; one cluster reads a single turned tile. Object generation costs a little more for each cluster. On WT avg_poland with every landclass de-tiled, it took 0.72 to 0.92 times the plain time at one cluster and 1.14 to 1.15 times at eight. Still, the smallest count that hides the period is the right one.

2 · How it works

Clusters, and a noise that picks between them

A cluster is the same tile, turned by some angle and shifted by some offset. Cluster 0 is the tile exactly as you authored it, turned to detileMainDir. The other clusters take one slice of the spread each, jittered inside it, and an offset from a sequence built to spread evenly, so no two come out near copies of each other. Every machine computes the same clusters from these keys, so no table is saved with the landclass.

A smooth world noise runs across the map. It picks which cluster shows, and near a transition it blends two of them. Left panel below is that map, one colour per cluster, dimmed where two of them blend, with an arrow on the tile axis. Right panel is the same ground, textured.

Plate 2 · the cluster map and the ground it makes noise 497 m - 5 clusters - dir 20, spread 60 deg

Every marker sits on the same painted ring inside the tile. That ring moves and turns with its cluster, which is the point: the objects read the cluster map the pixels read.

Four things follow from this.

  • A pixel reads at most two clusters, whatever the count. The ground costs the same with eight as with two.
  • The blend band is wide. 40% of the noise range shows a single cluster; the other 60% blends two. So most of your ground is a blend, not a corner case. More clusters switch more often, in narrower bands spread over the noise range.
  • The objects follow. Tiled entities and planted objects with a density map take the cluster that shows where they stand, and turn with it. A tree authored on a tile stays on its tile.
  • The clusters do not get equal ground. The noise spends most of its time near the middle of its range, so from three clusters up the middle ones cover most of the map and the first and last are rare; two clusters split it about evenly. With six clusters the engine selftest counts about 3% of the map for cluster 0. Raise the slider above and watch the map: every cluster's share narrows, and the new cluster takes the top of the noise range, the rare ground the last cluster held.

3 · The keys

Three keys in the asset, one in the level

The three landclass keys go in the detail{} block of <name>.land.blk, next to the size you already set. Leave them out and the landclass renders exactly as it does today.

// <name>.land.blk
detail {
  texture:t="dunes_tex_d"
  size:p2=512,512            // the tile size you already have, in metres

  detileClusters:i=3        // 0 off (default), 1 plain turn, 2..8 de-tiled
  detileMainDir:r=30         // degrees, the world direction of the tile U axis
  detileAngleSpread:r=20     // degrees, how far cluster 1 and up may turn off that direction
}

In War Thunder the keys reach the ground of a simple, detailed or detailed_r landclass; the DNG games de-tile the simple and the detailed ones only. It is the colormap that de-tiles: a detailed landclass's own detail texture and flowmap keep their tiling. On a mega (splattingmap) or an indexed (custom shader) landclass the keys are turned off when the asset loads, with an error in the tools console and in the game log, so its ground and its objects both stay plain: set them on a landclass whose ground can show them. The harness can still force one on with lc.detile or landClassOverrides: its combined shader covers the non-indexed mega variants too, so a mega tile de-tiles there and not in the game.

KeyRangeDefaultWhat it does
detileClusters 0–8 0 How many copies of the tile the landclass shows. 0 is off. 1 turns the tile to detileMainDir and nothing else. Start at 4 and raise only while the repeat still shows: each cluster adds a little object generation time. A value outside the range is clamped and logs an error.
detileMainDir degrees 0 The world direction of the tile U axis, measured from world +X toward world +Z. Cluster 0 sits exactly here. This is where you point the wind, the plough or the slope.
detileAngleSpread degrees 180 How wide a sector clusters 1 and up may turn into, plus or minus. Each takes one stratum of it, so the farthest reaches the spread times 1 - 0.5 / (clusters - 1): at 2 clusters a 30 degree spread turns the copy by at most 15. Small values keep a directional texture pointing one way.
size existing metres 1 Your tile size. It is also the range the cluster offsets are drawn from. size of zero or less keeps de-tiling off, whatever detileClusters says.
landDetileNoiseSize
per level, not per landclass		 metres 497 How far one cluster holds before another takes over. One value for the whole level, shared by every landclass and by the object generators. Change it and the objects move.

Why 497 and not 512. The default noise size is deliberately not a power of two. A round number would beat against a power-of-two tile size and put the cluster changes on a visible rhythm.

Setting it up

  1. Open <name>.land.blk and find its detail{} block. Check size:p2 first. It is the tile period in metres and the whole map is built on it, so a wrong size de-tiles at the wrong scale.
  2. Add detileClusters:i=4 and look at it. Four is what the harness gives a simple, detailed or detailed_r landclass whose asset sets no count. Raise it only while the repeat still shows.
  3. For a directional texture add detileMainDir, keep the count at 2 to 4, and give it a small detileAngleSpread.
  4. Rebuild the asset. The keys ride inside detail{}, so daBuild copies them with the block and no level is re-exported. The daEditor reads the asset source, so reloading the asset is enough there.
  5. Set landDetileNoiseSize:r in the level blk only if 497 m does not suit the level. It applies to every landclass of that level.
  6. Look at the result in the daEditor viewport or in the harness. In the harness, tune with lc.detile and lc.noise without a rebuild, then write the values you settled on back into the asset.

4 · Recipe · a directional tile

Dunes that keep one wind

Barchan dunes are the hard case. Every crest reads as the same wind, so a cluster that turns 90 degrees does not hide the repeat, it breaks the desert. The fix is a narrow detileAngleSpread around the wind direction. The clusters then differ mostly by their offsets, which is enough to break the period, and every crest still reads as the same wind.

Keep the count low here, 2 to 4. Offsets do the work, so more clusters buy little for the generation time each one adds.

Move detileAngleSpread below. The rose on the right is the actual cluster angles, not a drawing. Watch the crests stop agreeing somewhere past 40 degrees.

The dune tile is drawn here procedurally: crests across the wind, a lit windward slope and a dark lee. It is directional on purpose, so a wrong spread shows immediately.

What to paste

Start at 10 degrees and open up only if the repeat still reads.

// sand dunes: the wind runs along world +X
detail {
  texture:t="your_colormap"      // your own, unchanged
  size:p2=512,512
  detileClusters:i=3          // 2..4 for a directional tile
  detileMainDir:r=0          // point this along the wind you painted
  detileAngleSpread:r=10     // 10..30 keeps the crests reading as one wind
}
  • 10 degrees is the tight setting. Use it for barchan dunes, ripples, anything with a crest and a shadow.
  • 25 degrees is the open setting for a wider desert where the crests are softer.
  • Above about 40 degrees a dune tile starts to fight itself, and the blend band shows crests crossing.
  • Same rule for leaning grass, wind-combed steppe, water ripples: point detileMainDir at the direction and keep the spread small.

Setting only the direction. detileClusters:i=1 turns the tile to detileMainDir and does nothing else. It aligns a texture to the world. It hides no repeat.

5 · Recipe · an isotropic tile

Forest, where any angle is a good angle

A canopy from above has no direction, so it takes the easy setting: four to six clusters and the default 180 degree spread. You write one key. Start at four and go up only if the repeat still reads, because each cluster adds a little generation time for the trees.

The interesting part is the trees. A forest landclass plants rendInst objects from the same tile, and those follow the same clusters, as does a group planted through a density map. A planted group with neither scatters evenly and has no tile to follow. Turn de-tiling off below and the tree markers snap back to one grid, lined up with the texture repeat. Turn it on and both move together.

Plate 4 · canopy, and the rendInsts on it 3000 m across - tile 512 m

Markers stand for tiled entities and for planted objects that use a density map. Planted objects with no density map scatter evenly, so they have no tile to follow and nothing changes for them.

// forest canopy: isotropic, so the spread stays at its default
detail {
  texture:t="your_colormap"      // your own, unchanged
  size:p2=512,512
  detileClusters:i=4
}

The same one-key setting fits gravel, grass, scrub, rubble, snow and any other tile with no readable direction. Four to six is the working range. Two hide little, and past six the ground gains almost nothing while the generation cost keeps rising.

6 · The limit

Structured tiles double-expose

De-tiling blends two turned copies of one texture. If the eye continues the pattern, it sees both copies at once. Ploughed fields, paving, orchard rows, brickwork, anything with a straight edge that runs: the blend band turns into a double exposure, and it takes 60% of the noise range.

Below is a field tile with furrows and a track. Switch the band overlay on to see exactly where the two copies cross.

Plate 5 · a structured tile, de-tiled badly 2200 m across - tile 512 m

Even the 10 degree version does not save it. A straight furrow shifted against itself still reads as two furrows. The answer for a structured tile is to leave detileClusters out, and break the repeat with masks, decals or a second landclass instead.

The blend band is not a thin seam. It takes 60% of the noise range, so this failure is the normal case for a structured tile, not an edge case.

The test before you turn it on. Cover half the tile with the other half, turned 90 degrees, and look at the seam. If you can still tell where one ends, the tile is structured. Do not de-tile it.

7 · Where you see it

Three places, and what each one shows

AssetViewer

The land plugin draws the ground the generated objects stand on and fills it with your colormap at the landclass size and offset, where the land shader starts it, so the repeat reads there as it does on terrain. It opens on one whole tile. Its property panel edits the polygon edge size (up to the 8 km square the preview generates objects in), the preview center with a center on a tile button, the presentation shape, and, for a landclass whose ground follows de-tiling, carries a final look checkbox and shows the three keys read-only. Save the land.blk and the panel and the preview reload with the new values; a new tile size or offset opens the view on a tile again.

Leave the checkbox off and the preview is the plain landclass: the plain tile, with the objects on the plain grid. That is the pattern you are tiling, at the scale you set it. Turn it on and the plugin composes the de-tiled ground on the CPU from the same clusters and the same noise the shader uses, and generates the objects on those clusters while that ground is shown (during a size drag both stay plain), so you tune the keys against the picture the game gives. One thing the preview cannot know is the level: it lays the pattern out at the default 497 m noise size, so on a level that sets another landDetileNoiseSize the patches are larger or smaller than this, while the three keys read the same. A landclass that generates the polygon's own surface gets no checkbox: that surface is drawn with its own material, which the keys never reach, so its objects stay on the plain grid. Grass is the other exception: the preview plants it on the plain grid, while in the game the grass mask follows the keys. A mega or an indexed landclass gets no checkbox either: its keys are turned off.

The daEditor viewport

The terrain in the daEditor viewport does de-tile, and the object generators and the colour bake follow it. Two things to know while you work there.

  • The editor reads the level blk, so a level's landDetileNoiseSize reaches the viewport, the generators and the colour bake; a project with no level blk gets the 497 metre default.
  • Colour generation follows de-tiling for world-mapped images that are not clamped. Layered colour generation and grass placement do not follow yet.

The testLandClass harness

This is where you tune without a re-export. It lives in samples/testLandClass: build prog with jam, compile its shaders (../../../../tools/dagor_cdk/windows-x86_64/dsc2-dx12-dev.exe ./shaders_dx12.blk -shaderOn in prog/shaders, which writes game/compiledShaders/gameDX12), run the exe in game, and point it at a level through settings.blk or a -config override. Then edit the de-tiling live from the console and look.

CommandWhat it does
lc.listPrint every landclass with its current de-tiling.
lc.detile <name|*> <clusters> [dir] [spread]Edit one landclass, or all of them with *. Direction and spread are degrees. This is the fast loop.
lc.noise <metres>Set the level noise size.
lc.debug [0|1]Tint the clipmap by landclass family: red de-tiled, green mega, blue indexed.
lc.checker [0|1]Swap every tile for a red and green checker, which shows the repeat and the cluster edges plainly. Indexed landclasses keep theirs: their textures are indices, not tiles.
 
// a tuning session, in order
lc.list
lc.detile * 0                 // everything back to plain, to compare
lc.detile egypt_sand_dunes 3 0 10        // a name lc.list printed, not this one
lc.noise 497

To make it stick across launches, put it in the harness settings.blk instead:

level:t="levels/avg_poland.bin"  // the level to load, under the harness basePath
landDetileNoiseSize:r=497
landClassOverrides{
  egypt_sand_dunes{        // your landclass
    detileClusters:i=3
    detileMainDir:r=0
    detileAngleSpread:r=10
  }
}

Where the asset sets no count, the harness de-tiles a simple, detailed or detailed_r landclass with four clusters, so a level always starts with something to look at. An override wins by name.

What this page fakes

  • Every tile here is drawn in your browser from procedural noise. No shipping texture is used, and a real asset will read differently.
  • The de-tiling map is not faked. The hash, the 64×64 noise lookup table, the node weights and the cluster offsets are ported from the engine, and the strip below runs them against the values the rendInstMath selftest holds. No pin covers the cluster rotation: this page turns with Math.sin and Math.cos, the engine with vecmath's polynomial, so the two differ by about a ulp.
  • The blend uses each tile's own mean. The shader reads the coarsest mip of the colormap, which is the same quantity by another route.
  • The view is a flat top-down map with no terrain, no lighting, no clipmap mip selection and no streaming. Real ground is seen at an angle, and the repeat reads differently there.
  • The AssetViewer figure is a schematic drawn from the plugin source, not a screenshot.
  • Markers stand for generated objects, but they are not real rendInst assets. Each tiled marker keeps the cluster the texture shows at its spot, the rule of the tiled generator, though the page walks every cluster where the generator walks only those that can show; planted markers are schematic.
